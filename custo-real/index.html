Case study · Browser extension

Custo Real

The real cost of a used car, right on the listing. A Chrome extension that shows, on every car listing on Standvirtual, an estimate of the annual road tax (IUC) and the monthly fuel cost, without opening a single calculator.

01 · The problem

The price on the listing is only part of the cost.

When you buy a used car in Portugal you also pay the IUC road tax every year, plus fuel every month. Both depend on details buried in the listing: the date of first registration, engine size, CO₂ emissions, fuel type and consumption.

Comparing cars meant copying those values into separate calculators, one listing at a time. Custo Real does those calculations on the listing itself, as soon as the page opens.

03 · Features

What it does

Road tax with the breakdown

Annual IUC estimate with each part of the calculation: engine size, CO₂, year coefficient and the diesel surcharge.

Monthly fuel cost

Uses the consumption from the listing, or a default one, with the kilometres per month and the prices set in the options.

Live fuel prices

Petrol, diesel and LPG prices come from the government's DGEG portal, averaged by district and cached for 12 hours, with manual values as a fallback.

Honest about missing data

When a value is missing it says which one instead of inventing it. Without CO₂ it shows the possible range and lets you type the value.

Edge cases covered

Electric cars (exempt), cars registered before 1981, imported cars, hybrids, LPG and commercial vehicles, each with its own message.

Doesn't break the site

The panel lives in a Shadow DOM, so it neither inherits Standvirtual's styles nor affects its layout, and it can be minimised.

Three separate layers

The adapter is the only part that knows Standvirtual. Supporting another site means adding one file in src/sites; the normaliser and the calculations stay the same.

Rules as data

The tax tables live in a data-only file. Updating them for a new year means copying one file and changing the values, without touching the logic.

No build, no frameworks

Plain JavaScript with Manifest V3. The whole extension is small, easy to review and fast to load.

05 · Technical decisions

Problems and how I solved them

Data

Reading the listing reliably

Problem: reading the visible text breaks whenever the site changes its layout.

Solution: the adapter reads the JSON the site embeds in the page (__NEXT_DATA__). When the site switches listings without reloading, the extension requests the HTML of the open listing to read its data.

Rules

Getting the tax right

Problem: the official IUC tables are published as images, and the rules depend on the registration date, fuel and emissions standard.

Solution: I transcribed the tables from the IUC Code, cross-checked them with consumer association examples, and turned those official examples into tests.

Uncertainty

Showing a range instead of guessing

Problem: many listings leave out CO₂, don't say whether it was measured under NEDC or WLTP, or give only "2007" without the month.

Solution: the extension calculates every possible case and shows the range, explaining what is missing, instead of showing a precise but wrong number.

Privacy

Automatic prices, nothing collected

Problem: fuel prices change every week, but users shouldn't have to update them by hand or share anything.

Solution: a service worker reads the public DGEG prices at most every 12 hours, without sending anything about the user or the listing. Permissions are limited to the two sites it needs.

06 · Technical honesty

Limitations and next steps

  • Estimates, not the final bill. Listings often lack CO₂, and the advertised consumption is the official figure, usually lower than real-world use.
  • Not covered yet. Heavier commercial vehicles, motorbikes, taxis and some exemptions; the panel says so instead of estimating.
  • Yearly update. The tax tables are the 2026 ones and have to be updated by hand when the rules change.
  • Next versions. The panel next to the price, OLX support, total cost over 3 years, alerts for suspicious mileage or prices, and side-by-side comparison.

07 · Takeaways

What I learned

  • Turning legal rules into code and tests, and checking every value against official sources.
  • Working with data I don't control: other people's pages and a public government service.
  • Taking a product from idea to store: privacy policy, store listing and review.
